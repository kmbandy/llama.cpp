Authored by grok. `hipGraphExecUpdate` is not a safe pointer-patch: s0 took two

remote one — becomes an infinite relaunch loop. Two ~25-minute loops today
(12:13–12:22, 12:56–13:07), each broken only by killing the remote workers by
hand. Now SIGTERMs both sides first.

### 3.1 The accept-length cliff is the GATE, not the head

Settles §2.2 of the knobs doc and overturns the 2026-08-16 "suspected
driver-level defect" conclusion. At conf-min=0 the draft chain reaches position 5
(mean_len 2.76); at **any** nonzero floor it dies after position 1 — position 3
onward is exactly 0.000. Measured twice, the second time on a run that crashed
mid-arm and gave a near-identical curve (0.743, 0.486, 0.273, 0.191, 0.093 / 2.79).

### 3.2 But opening the gate is net-negative on a paged rig, −20%

conf-min=0 accepts 992/2685 = **37%** of drafted tokens versus 71% at 0.4. It
generates 2.5× the draft tokens to buy ~0.7 tokens of accept length, and every
wider verify pulls more expert sets through the pager. This is the MTP lesson
repeating — longer accepts can lose on a paged model — and is why acc/pos must be
read before t/s.

### 3.3 The gate is a cliff, not a gradient

0.2 / 0.4 / 0.6 are indistinguishable on every metric. There is no intermediate
floor that buys depth cheaply. **Kills "tune conf-min to a sweet spot."** Any win
here must come from making a *deeper* draft cheaper to verify — fewer expert sets
per verify — not from moving this knob.

### 3.4 A real but non-throughput far edge at 0.8–0.9

Position-2 acceptance forms two non-overlapping clusters: 0.2/0.4/0.4/0.6 give
0.261–0.278 (spread 0.017), while 0.8/0.9 give 0.192/0.199 (spread 0.007). Two
independent arms landing ~30% below a four-arm cluster that tight is an effect,
not scatter — a high floor does trim genuine position-2 accepts, with mean_len
tracking to 1.91–1.92. It costs no throughput, which fits: at ~2 tokens of accept
length the verify batch is already near its floor.

**Prefill was flat 27.08–28.78 across every arm** — the knob is decode-only in
effect. Config of record left at 0.4; 0.6 is nominally highest but inside drift,
so adopting it would be reading noise.

**Cross-validated by three independent instruments**, which is why the split is
trustworthy: spine target total 357.5 ms vs the s0 `WP_REQ_LOG` chain summed over
811 passes × 43 layers = 360.5 ms (0.8% apart); spine draft-head 79.5 ms vs the

**Instrument cost measured, not assumed:** 2.451 t/s instrumented vs 2.665/2.736

absolute ms are usable with that caveat.

### 4.1 How to read WP_DISPATCH_STATS on this rig

It emits one WARN per `llama_context::decode()`, and with DSpark there are **two
per verify cycle**. Separate them by the `layers=` field, **not** by `phase=` or
`n_tokens=`:

- `layers=43` → the target verify (n_tokens 2–4, the real expert chain)
- `layers=3` → the DSpark draft head (n_tokens=5, layers 43–45)

Both are labelled `phase=verify`. Pooling them halves every median and makes the
budget irreconcilable — the first pass produced "dispatch total 208 ms", which
contradicted s0's own 360 ms log and was the tell that the populations were mixed.

### 4.2 What it means for 10 t/s

Using this arm's own numbers (s0 resident request 2.906 ms vs 8.835 ms median,
33.4% resident):

| scenario | cycle | t/s |
|---|---|---|
| as measured | 767 ms | 2.45 |
| perfect prefetch (all s0 resident) | ~491 ms | 3.83 |
| all expert dispatch eliminated | 330 ms | 5.70 |

A 10 t/s cycle is 188 ms. The spine-side block alone is 330 ms, so it has to be
attacked for the target to be reachable. Prime suspect is on record: eager
per-node launch of the 8598-node decode graph (~80 ms/token) — the DSV4 KV-cache
shape-invariance rewrite that would let spine HIP-graph capture hold.

Secondary: pack/issue/unpack are noise (0.11 / 8.85 / 0.15 ms).
`first_await_in_flight avg=3.0, max_in_flight=3` of 5 workers — per-layer worker
fan-out is topology, not pipeline slack, consistent with 2026-07-30.

## 5. DSPARK_ON_GPU — the head pinned on the slice legs

**What it is.** Serve DSpark layers 43–45 from the three sliced GPU legs, pinned
resident, instead of from the two full-width CPU workers d0/d1.

**Why it was cheap.** The width-sliced data already existed on all three legs and
was simply not being loaded. The plain manifest covers layers 0..45 (11776
groups); the `-dspark` variant covers 0..42 (11008). The suffix means "the variant
for when a separate dspark worker exists", i.e. it **excludes** the head — and the
harness had been launching the excluding variant. So: a manifest swap plus a
layer-keyed pin, not a reshard.

Pinning was likewise already implemented and layer-keyed:
`--weight-paging-resident-experts` takes block ranges (`wp-router.cpp:139`) and
`select_victim` consults `reserve_blocks_` by `page.layer`
(`wp-expert-worker.cpp:3198`). The dispatcher already supports both shapes —
`layer_slice_mode` is per-layer "not global", and
`pipe-expert-dispatcher.cpp:637` names this exact mixed-fleet case.

**What the CPU head was.** Not fully resident, as first assumed from directory
size — a **paged pool**: d0 85 slots / 1.06 GiB, d1 171 slots / 2.13 GiB, slot
12.75 MiB = one expert-layer, 256 slots against 768 expert-layer pages ≈ **33%
residency**, the same coverage ratio as s0. Experts are MXFP4 (4.25 bpv, exactly
13,369,344 B/expert-layer), *not* bf16 and not Q8 — the descriptor names a Q8_0
source GGUF but the eshard pipeline requantized.

**Config applied** (env-gated `DSPARK_ON_GPU=1`, one flag to revert): plain
manifest on all three legs, `--weight-paging-resident-experts 43-45`, d0/d1 not
launched, INI `expert-dispatch` trimmed to the three GPU legs, port-wait and
readiness gate no longer wait on 8807/8808. Spec caps deliberately **unchanged**
so the pin is one variable.

**Verified applied:** `layers=46 slots=2682` (3450 − 768) and
`pinned_pages=768 pinned_bytes=7,059,065,856` on s0; d0/d1 not listening.

| arm | decode | mean_len | acc/pos | prefill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pinned, conf 0.4 | 2.686 | 1.92 | 0.713, 0.211 | 27.88 |
| pinned, conf 0.6 | 2.657 | 1.93 | 0.687, 0.242 | 27.09 |

Pooled: pinned **2.67** vs CPU-head **2.73** — ~2%, inside the drift bracket. The
two pinned arms agree to 1.1%, tighter than the CPU arms agree among themselves.
**Throughput-neutral, at both gate settings.** Raising the gate did not unlock it.

**Mechanism, and why it cancels:**

| | CPU head | pinned |
|---|---|---|
| s0 chain | 360.5 ms / 43 layers | 425.8 ms / 46 layers |
| head cost | 79.5 ms, separate stage | 65.3 ms, inside the chain |
| s0 residency | 33.4% | 29.0% |

The head did get cheaper (−14 ms), its page-ins are gone, and two loopback round
trips plus the 3900X left the critical path. But s0 gave up 768 slots for it and
the main 43 layers got correspondingly slower. The two effects cancel.

**Prefill held at 27.09–27.88 despite a 22% cut to s0's paging pool** — the one
clearly good surprise, and evidence that prefill is not capacity-bound here.

**Why the lead time did not convert.** `spec_cap[pending/blocked_budget]` is still
`0/0` and `spec_dropped` rose 989 → 1187. Speculation still never fills even 891
slots, so the pager is not slot-limited — it is limited by hints that survive the
scorer. Earlier hints arrived and were discarded. Raising the spec caps is
therefore still a no-op **on this configuration**; it becomes a live lever only
once the hint path stops dropping.

## 8. Speculative page-in depth — BUILT, NOT MEASURED (open)

`WP_EXPERT_SPEC_MAX_INFLIGHT` (commits `768533d41`, `ef09ca6d9`), on both boxes,
unit-tested, default 1. **No valid throughput measurement exists.** Do not cite
any depth number from this session.

### Why it was built

The speculative page-in path was single-batch serialized: `spec_batch_` was one
`unique_ptr`, submit refused while it was set, and the pump bailed to
`pump_vram_busy_` whenever it was live. Measured on the config of record:

```
pump[calls/gated/hbusy/hempty/hsubmit/hfiltered/vbusy/vempty/vsubmit]
   = 2494007/43/0/2493964/0/0/2484065/8458/1441
spec_queue_left=51   spec_cap[pending/blocked_budget]=16/0
```

2.48M blocked pump calls against 1441 submits, with pages queued throughout, and
`blocked_budget=0` against an 891 cap because at most ~8 pages were ever in
flight. That is why K=3/7/15 crossed with conf 0.4/0.75/0.8 all returned
2.686-2.697: those knobs change what gets QUEUED, and the queue drained at one
`SPEC_CHUNK` batch per completion.

### The numbers, and why none of them are usable

| binary | depth | measurement | reps |
|---|---|---|---|
| `8c3c5a13f` | n/a | 2.686 warm | 3 |
| `ef09ca6d9` | 1 | 2.514 warm | **1** |
| `ef09ca6d9` | 4 | 2.215 warm | **1** |
| `ef09ca6d9` | 1 | 2.099 after a worker cycle | 1, cold pool |

**depth=1 must reproduce 2.686 and does not.** Until that is resolved every other
depth number is void, including the apparent 4-vs-1 gap. A 1-rep number is not
comparable to a 3-rep mean, and the like-for-like `REPS=3` run at depth=1 was
never completed.

### Code audit, for whoever picks this up

I read every behavioural line the diff removed. At cap=1 I could NOT find a
difference from the old path:

- **Interlock** (`ensure_batch` ~2306): old called `spec_pagein_poll(true)`;
  new calls `spec_pagein_poll(false, page)`. Looks like a blocking->non-blocking
  change but is not: `wait_for` promotes `must_block` to true for the batch
  holding the page, the two match predicates are identical (`layer`+`expert`),
  and at cap=1 there is one batch which by construction holds it.
- **Submit filter** (~2769): the resident / already-in-slot skip survived. The
  added `spec_in_flight_for` clause is a no-op at cap=1 (submit refuses entry
  while any batch is live, so nothing can be in flight when the filter runs).
- **Pump gate, poll loop, retire-by-index, budget clamp**: all collapse to the
  old control flow at size <= 1.

So either the 6% is rep-count noise, or the difference is somewhere I did not
look. THE NEXT STEP IS THE MEASUREMENT, NOT MORE READING: `REPS=3` at depth=1 on
a warm pool, same protocol as the 2.686 arm, no worker cycle in between.

### Two rig facts that cost hours today, worth carrying

**Cycling workers under a live spine breaks serving.** The spine holds persistent
sockets; after a worker-only cycle it sends into dead ones and every request
returns `Compute error` / `ret = -3` with the worker healthy and listening. Use
`--cycle-all`. A worker-knob sweep MUST cycle the router too.

**A 32-token warmup cannot warm a 2682-slot pool.** After a worker cycle the
first 512-token run reads ~20% low. Warm with a full discarded 512 before
measuring, or don't cycle workers between arms.

### 8.3 Why NO knob moves decode — per-layer serial floor (measured)

Two independent instruments, same answer.

**s0 `WP_REQ_LOG`, depth=1 measured run** (12,133 requests, 264 verify cycles,
179 s): cycle = 678 ms carrying mean_len ≈ 1.94 tokens. s0 busy 335 ms (49%);
s0 idle 343 ms (51%). The idle is NOT one end-of-cycle block: segmented by
request boundaries it is **7.3 ms of spine turnaround at every one of the ~45
layer boundaries (327 ms/cycle)**, with only 17 ms between cycles. Inside s0's
335 ms: page-in wait 185 ms (residency already 79.7%), compute ~104 ms
(hits 62 + pagein 42), fixed overheads the rest. Worker graph cache now replays
(gcache 262 hit / 5 miss), h2d 10.9 GB/s.

**Spine `forward-budget.log` (08-18 capture, CPU-head config), target pass
only (43 layers, warm):** wall 716 ms/cycle = **376 ms unpack/fold of worker
partials (8.75 ms/layer, spine CPU)** + **328 ms "other" = dense compute +
eager per-node graph launch (7.6 ms/layer)**; pack/issue ~0.

So the decode cycle is a serial per-layer loop, ~15–17 ms/layer × 46 layers,
split roughly worker-service / spine-unpack / spine-dense-launch — divided by
an accept length stuck at ~2. **Every knob in stackd-worker.env (depth, conf,
K, caps, head placement) tunes speculative I/O inside the worker's 185 ms
slice — 27% of the cycle.** Zeroing page-in wait entirely caps at ~4 t/s.
Nothing in that knob family touches the spine's ~330 ms (unpack + eager dense
launch) or the 46-serial-RTT structure. That is why the lever board is flat.

What can move decode, in measured-size order: (1) spine unpack/fold off the
critical path or overlapped with next-layer dispatch — 376 ms/cycle; (2) the
DSV4 shape-invariance rewrite so spine HIP-graph capture holds — inside the
328 ms; (3) accept length — multiplies everything; (4) prefetch accuracy (the
§7 hint path) — worth at most the 185 ms wait slice.

### 8.2 Root cause of tonight's env clobbering — found and fixed

`dep.sh`'s config-of-record restore step was
`open(p,'w').writelines([l for l in open(p) ...])` — the write-mode open
**truncates the file before the read runs**, so every completed sweep zeroed
`stackd-worker.env` (155-line config of record → empty; workers then launched
with defaults: no spec caps, no host victim, no HIP graphs, no hint log). Every
worker arm measured between the first truncation (~19:18) and 21:20 ran that

The probe costs four times what it measures — but differencing recovers the answer.

A six-stage poll decomposition ran across 27 real GEMM shapes — 162 dispatches, two RTC stamps per stage. The stage numbers came back clean and consistent. They are dominated by the instrument, and the first headline drawn from them was wrong. Differencing against a near-null stage recovers a sharper result than the one it replaces. Everything below is marked by what it rests on.

Same wall time, one fifth the loop iterations. The probe build does the same amount of externally-visible work in the same span while executing 5× fewer passes — so each pass carries roughly 3.8× its own weight in instrument.

Stage 1 forces the conclusion. It brackets three source lines, and deadman_check is throttled — on 63 of 64 passes it executes exactly three SALU instructions:

s_add_u32    s71, s71, 1
s_cmp_ge_u32 s71, DEADMAN_EVERY
s_cbranch_scc0 .Ldm_skip

Measured: 487 ns. Three ALU ops are not 487 ns. pollstage_enter and leave are two s_sendmsg_rtn RTC reads on the SQ front-end message bus, serialised across 2048 waves — the exact resource the kernel’s own rule-5 comments warn about.

Consequence for the headline. A large constant added to every stage compresses their shares toward each other. The “phase profile is identical on every shape, CV 0.09–0.16” result is most likely measuring the probe’s uniformity, not the kernel’s. The true spread is wider by an unknown amount.

The check that failed. Probe cost was judged by span — 0.99×, which reads as free. Span was the wrong health metric; pass count was the right one, and it says 0.21×.

Recovered · differenced against a null

Valid within the equal-rate group

Stage 1 brackets three SALU ops. It is a null calibration, so subtracting it cancels the shared probe.

Stages 1, 2, 5 and 6 all fire the probe at the same rate (f ≈ 2.0 sendmsg per pass), so they sit in the same contention regime and can be compared to each other. Stage 1’s bracketed work is three SALU instructions on 63 of 64 passes — effectively zero. Whatever it measures is the instrument.

Stages 3 and 4 fire at 1.12 and 0.23 per pass. Different contention, different scale — they cannot be placed on this axis at all.

This sharpens the original claim rather than restoring it

The first version of this page said 54% failed-peek-plus-park, 42% watchdog-and-gate. Corrected: the watchdog and the gate cost nothing. The failed reservation attempt and the park that follows are essentially all of the measurable per-pass work, split about 2:1.

The 42% previously attributed to stages 1 and 2 was entirely instrument.

What this rests on: that stage 1’s bracketed work is ~0 (supported by the source), and that contention is matched inside the group. Absolute values remain inflated by the shared offset — only the differences are trustworthy. “+390 ns” is a difference against a null, not an absolute cost.

A general correction is not possible, and that is settled.

Ablation · no probe at all

Valid · strongest result of the day

4 arms × 27 shapes. Median change vs control; positive means slower without the work.

armwhat was deletedmedian Δ
NOWMMAevery WMMA deleted+1.6%
NODSADDLDS reduction deleted+1.7%

Noise floor ~1.2%. Three of four arms run slower without the work. Deleting all the math, the LDS reduction, the output stores and the entire B memory stream changes nothing, on every shape, across 18× in K and 2,700× in tile count. No instrumentation involved — each arm is a build with code removed and a wall-clock span.

162 phase dispatches + 108 ablation + 27 control. All work-exact with oracle bad=0, zero hangs, zero GPU resets. Every figure generated directly from ~/dsws_gpu_logs/{ps_s*,abl2_*,c512ctl_*}.log — none transcribed by hand.

The standing lesson, now twice-learned: ablate, don’t probe. Where a probe is unavoidable, judge its cost by work completed, not by elapsed time.
